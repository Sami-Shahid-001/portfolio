01

The problem

Businesses wanted a phone line that never goes to voicemail, an agent that answers, qualifies, books and follows up. Building one means stitching together telephony, streaming speech-to-text, a language model, a voice, a knowledge base, a CRM and a billing model that charges for every second of all of it.

Agencies wanted something one level up: to sell that capability to their own clients, under their own brand, at their own prices, without those clients ever learning which platform sits underneath.

So Ferra had to be two products at once. A no-code builder simple enough for a dental clinic, and a white-label, multi-tenant platform strict enough that one partner’s client can never see, or be billed for, another’s.

02

The voice loop

Every call is a loop of turns. Caller audio streams into speech-to-text; endpointing decides whether the caller has finished or only paused; the transcript goes to the language model with the agent’s prompt, tools and retrieved knowledge; the reply streams into text-to-speech and back down the line.

The hard part is not any single step, it is the latency budget across all of them. A pause that feels natural in a chat window feels broken on a phone. Barge-in matters as much as speed: when the caller interrupts, playback stops and the loop listens again instead of talking over them.

My part was the agent workflows themselves, prompts, tool calls into CRMs and calendars, inbound and outbound call flows, and wiring the voice and LLM APIs to the platform’s REST endpoints so that a call could book an appointment, sync a contact and hand off to SMS or WhatsApp.

03

Providers that fail over

Each agent version points at a routing profile: a primary provider and an ordered list of fallbacks. A router owns failover, circuit breaking and a cost ceiling; adapters own each provider’s wire format. Neither knows about the other’s concerns, so adding a provider never means touching the call orchestrator.

Credentials resolve through a vault in a fixed order, agent, then client, then partner, then the platform’s pooled keys. A partner can bring their own OpenAI or ElevenLabs account and every call on their tenants uses it; everyone else falls back to the pool.

04

White-label by Host header

The dashboard resolves branding from the request’s Host header. A partner who points app.theirbrand.com at the platform gets their own logo, colours and product name on every screen, and their clients never see the word Ferra.

That single decision shaped the architecture. A partner’s domain cannot be a path under ferra.ai, and a framework’s base path is fixed at build time, so the marketing site and the product became separate applications on separate hostnames, sharing one session cookie on the parent domain.

The links between the two apps are deliberately asymmetric. Marketing links into the product generously; the product links back to marketing only for the platform’s own brand. On a partner’s domain that link is simply not rendered, one “Back to Ferra” would tell their clients whose platform they are on.

05

Tenants that cannot see each other

Isolation is enforced by construction rather than by remembering to add a WHERE clause. Every tenant-scoped query goes through a scoped database builder bound to the caller’s resolved tenant; the tenant predicate is appended by the builder and cannot be omitted or overridden.

  1. A row’s tenant is immutable, an update that tries to move it between tenants throws.
  2. Raw SQL against a tenant table must carry an explicit tenant token, or it does not run.
  3. A test fails the build if a new table with a tenant column is not registered with the guard.
  4. Asking for another tenant’s call by id returns 404, not 403, for you, the record does not exist.
  5. Permissions are re-read from the membership on every request rather than trusted from the token, so revoking access lands within a minute instead of when a token happens to expire.

06

Paying by the minute

Pricing is pay-as-you-go from a prepaid wallet. Every call is metered per leg, speech-to-text, language model and text-to-speech each carry their own usage and cost, so the call detail shows exactly where the money went, and a partner can set their own rates for their clients and keep the margin.

Metering cannot be decided by the browser. The streaming test console reaches the voice agent through a small relay that holds no billing logic of its own: it forwards the session for authorisation and reports usage back to the dashboard, and each report is bound to a signed ticket issued at authorisation, so a turn cannot be billed to a different tenant.

08

The affiliate programme

Research into the competitor Ferra was benchmarked against showed it had not built an affiliate programme at all, it resold a hosted one. Ferra’s was built in-house instead: affiliate accounts, an admin review queue, referral links and click tracking.

Commission

20% recurring, for the lifetime of every customer an affiliate refers.

Payouts are deliberately the last phase, gated on the billing integration, because commission arithmetic is only as trustworthy as the subscription events it is computed from.

09

What the audit measured

The platform carries its own audit harness that signs in as every seeded role and walks the product. Its last recorded run: 64 checks, 59 passed, 5 warnings, none failed. Four of the warnings are correct behaviour, partner-only pages redirecting for a client admin, and the fifth is the live voice leg, which the harness does not exercise.

10

What holds, and what does not

What holds: tenant isolation is enforced by construction and tested; provider failover and per-leg metering are real code paths with real traces; white-labelling survives every screen a client can reach.

What does not, yet: the local build runs on SQLite with an application-level guard standing in for PostgreSQL row-level security; circuit breakers and concurrency caps live in one process rather than across nodes; and affiliate payouts wait on billing. The move to production infrastructure adds those beneath the same code paths rather than replacing them.
