Neural Networks from Scratch

Before leaning on frameworks, I wrote the maths myself. Three NumPy-only projects: multivariate linear regression with gradient descent, a dense network on spiral data, and an MNIST digit recogniser with full backpropagation, served through Streamlit.

01

Why from scratch

Frameworks hide exactly the parts that break: shapes, gradients, scaling, numerical stability. Writing them by hand once makes every later model easier to debug, because you know what the framework is doing on your behalf.

02

Linear regression

  1. Generate a synthetic dataset with a known linear relationship plus noise.
  2. Normalise every feature, and the target, by mean and standard deviation.
  3. Compute the mean-squared-error cost for the current weights.
  4. Take the gradient and update the weights with gradient descent until the cost converges.

Multivariate regression first, because every later idea, a cost, its gradient, a learning rate, convergence, appears here in its simplest form.

03

A dense network on spirals

Next, a multi-class classifier on a spiral dataset that no straight line can separate: dense layers, ReLU and softmax activations and categorical cross-entropy loss, each written first in raw Python and then vectorised with NumPy. The focus was forward-pass correctness, the foundation the backward pass is built on.

04

MNIST with backpropagation

Then the full loop on MNIST: 70,000 handwritten digits, 60,000 for training and 10,000 for testing. Forward propagation, backpropagation, cross-entropy loss, weight initialisation and gradient-descent updates are all manual matrix operations.

The trained weights are serialised with pickle and served through a Streamlit app: upload a digit, see the prediction, an end-to-end machine-learning pipeline with no framework anywhere in it.

05

What it taught

  1. Most bugs are shape bugs, print the shapes before you print anything else.
  2. Softmax and cross-entropy together have a beautifully simple gradient.
  3. Normalising inputs is the difference between converging and wandering.
  4. Logits, probabilities and predictions are three different things, and confusing them is easy.
