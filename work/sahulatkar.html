SahulatKar BNPL Platform

A vendor-agnostic, Shariah-compliant Buy Now, Pay Later platform for Pakistan. Paste any product link, get a Murabaha financing offer, and an agent completes the purchase on the merchant’s own site with a single-use virtual card. I built the three frontends people actually touch.

01

The problem

Buy Now, Pay Later in Pakistan mostly works the way it does everywhere: a merchant integrates a provider, and you can only pay in instalments at the shops that did. Most conventional products also charge interest, which, for a large share of customers, rules them out entirely.

SahulatKar set out to remove both limits at once. Vendor-agnostic: any product, from any online store, with no merchant integration. Shariah-compliant: financing structured as Murabaha, a disclosed cost-plus sale, rather than an interest-bearing loan.

02

How a purchase works

A customer pastes the link to a product. The platform extracts it, prices a Murabaha offer, runs identity and credit checks, and has the customer sign two contracts in order, an agency agreement, then the Murabaha sale itself. Only then is a single-use virtual card issued, and a browser agent on the backend completes checkout on the merchant’s own page.

Profit is disclosed rather than hidden in a rate: tiered at 2.5%, 7% and 12% for 3-, 6- and 12-month plans, targeting a consistent effective markup of roughly 4% a year.

03

Six services, one door

Behind the frontends sit six independent services. Frontends talk to exactly one of them, the gateway, which owns authentication, KYC orchestration, the order lifecycle, contracts and the admin back office.

Gateway

  • The only public HTTP surface
  • Auth, KYC, orders, contracts
  • Admin back office and webhooks

Product service

  • URL to product extraction
  • Murabaha pricing
  • Autonomous checkout agent

Credit engine

  • Multi-layer risk scoring
  • Credit limits and down payments
  • Fraud and blacklist checks

Payments & ledger

  • Virtual cards, JazzCash, SafePay
  • Double-entry bookkeeping
  • Charity routing for late fees

That boundary mattered for frontend work more than anything else. Every screen I built had one contract to honour, a catalogue of 400 gateway endpoints, instead of six services’ worth of internal APIs.

04

Shariah rules, enforced in code

  1. The agency agreement must be signed before the Murabaha contract can even be generated.
  2. Contracts disclose the cost price and the profit amount explicitly.
  3. Late fees are never revenue, they post to a charity liability and are disbursed, never recognised as income.
  4. Prohibited categories are blocked at order time, twice: in the gateway and again in product extraction.
  5. Interface copy never says “interest”, “APR” or “loan repayment” where “profit”, “Murabaha instalment” or “financing” is accurate.

The last rule is the one the frontend owns completely. In a financing product, the words on the screen are part of the agreement the customer believes they are making.

05

The marketing site and early-access funnel

The first frontend had one job before the funding round: collect a thousand early-access signups. It is a Next.js 15 App Router site built mostly from server components, so almost no JavaScript ships for static content, with self-hosted fonts and no animation library at all. Every effect is a CSS keyframe or a scroll-driven view timeline.

Pages

  • Landing page with interactive phone mock-ups
  • Five “Why SahulatKar” pillar pages
  • About, contact and a legal hub

Funnel

  • Early-access signup
  • One-time email verification
  • A separate affiliate signup

Performance

  • Server components by default
  • Zero-dependency animation
  • Preloaded fonts, no layout shift

Goal

  • 1,000 early-access signups
  • Ahead of the funding round

06

The customer portal

The second frontend, SahulatKar Bazaar, is where a customer pastes a link, reviews an offer, signs and manages instalments. It was built against a stand-in gateway: a dependency-free Node process holding orders, carts and sessions in memory, deployed as one always-on service so the whole journey could be demonstrated before the real backend was.

Building against a mock is only honest if the mock matches the contract. It was written from the same endpoint catalogue and the backend’s own integration guide, so replacing it with the real gateway is a configuration change, not a rewrite.

07

The admin console

The third frontend is an operations console for a system that runs an autonomous agent buying real products on real websites, which fails in ways no human caused. Before building it, I rewrote its specification against reality: every screen grounded in the live gateway’s endpoints and nine backend audit documents, rather than a wishlist benchmarked from other BNPL products.

  1. Twelve real admin roles, with every screen annotated for exactly who can see it and act on it.
  2. Backend gaps flagged screen by screen, the interface degrades gracefully instead of inventing data.
  3. “Live” views are honest about polling: there is no push channel for admin screens, so the UI says how fresh its numbers are.
  4. Placeholders look different from facts. In a fintech console, an operator mistaking a hard-coded figure for a real one is the worst failure mode.

The console itself is React, TypeScript and Vite, a dense, data-first interface closer to a bank’s back office than a marketing page.

08

What the tests hold

The backend carries a genuine end-to-end test that brings up the whole stack, real PostgreSQL, real Redis, real networking and a real browser against a bundled mock merchant, and walks a customer from registration to a verified, balanced ledger entry with no mocking at all.

It is the most reliable reference for exact request and response shapes across the platform, and the frontends were checked against it. Building it surfaced real bugs that no mocked test could have caught, including a connection-pooler incompatibility that could silently drop financial postings.

09

What holds, and what does not

What holds: the three frontends are built against the real gateway contract, the Shariah wording rules are enforced in the interface, and the customer journey runs end to end against the stand-in gateway.

What does not, yet: SahulatKar is pre-launch. The customer portal is demonstrated against a mock that resets on every restart; admin screens that sit on backend gaps degrade rather than act; and whether contracts’ Shariah-board validation is backed by a recorded approval process is an open question on the backend, not something a frontend can settle.
